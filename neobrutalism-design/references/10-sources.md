# Sources and further reading

Source section: [Sources & Further Reading](https://neubrutalism.com/#sources)

The source page is a synthesis whose claims should be traceable to primary or authoritative secondary material. Use this catalogue to select the right evidence. Verify time-sensitive examples and contested attribution before citing them.

## Foundations and history

| Source | Type | Supports | Cautions |
|---|---|---|---|

## Sourcing methodology

The source page states that historical and standards claims are tied to primary materials. It checks brand examples against the live sites' rendered presentation rather than repeating commentary and removes examples when they no longer fit; Figma's 2024 rebrand is the cautionary case. Where attribution is disputed—especially who coined the digital term—it states uncertainty instead of manufacturing one origin story.

Apply the same method when using this skill:

- Prefer standards and first-party material for requirements, dates, names, and project status.
- Use authoritative secondary analysis for synthesis and design judgment.
- Use tertiary sources to discover leads, not settle contested claims.
- Timestamp brand observations, popularity counts, marketplace counts, and maintenance status.
- Distinguish visual adoption on a public surface from adoption across an entire product.
- Label inference and source-page synthesis explicitly.
