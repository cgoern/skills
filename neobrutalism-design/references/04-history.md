# History and provenance

Source section: [04 — History: From Concrete to CSS](https://neubrutalism.com/#history)

## Evidence boundary

The source presents neubrutalism as a lineage from postwar New Brutalist architecture through Italian radical anti-design, web brutalism, and the design and publishing platforms that made a visual tendency reproducible and marketable. Treat that lineage as an interpretive framework rather than a documented chain of direct influence. Dates and launches are often verifiable; claims that contemporary interface design “owes” qualities to a movement or that a platform caused crystallization are synthesis.

## Timeline

### 1950s–1960s: architectural New Brutalism

Hans Asplund reportedly used the Swedish term *nybrutalism* in 1950 after encountering Villa Göth in Uppsala. British architects later adopted the expression; Alison Smithson supplied an early published English use in 1953 for the Smithsons' unbuilt Soho house.

Reyner Banham's 1955 essay framed the emerging movement through memorability as an image, clear exhibition of structure, and valuation of materials in their “as found” condition. Banham revisited the subject in his 1966 book *The New Brutalism: Ethic or Aesthetic?* Alison and Peter Smithson were central advocates, but the name and movement developed across several people and contexts.

Do not state categorically that the name derives only from Le Corbusier's *béton brut*. Raw concrete was an important association, but the etymology also involves Asplund's Swedish word, British debates around New Humanism, and Banham's links to *béton brut* and Jean Dubuffet's *art brut*.

### 1960s–1980s: radical anti-design and Memphis

Italian Radical Architecture opposed modernist rationalism and functionalism. Archizoom and Superstudio used speculative, anti-establishment, playful work to challenge accepted ideas about architecture, objects, consumption, and society. Memphis, founded in 1981 around Ettore Sottsass and active until 1986, rejected conventional assumptions about function, material, surface, form, and color.

The source interprets neubrutalism's bright color, exaggeration, theatricality, and purposeful friction as owing as much to Italian anti-design as to concrete architecture. This is plausible formal analysis, not proof that later interface designers consciously derived the style from those groups.

### 2014–2016: web brutalism is named and showcased

Pascal Deville launched Brutalist Websites in 2014. The archive curated raw, unconventional sites and helped establish “web brutalism” as a critical label. By 2016, mainstream commentary treated it as resistance to homogeneous, polished startup design and repeated Deville's description of it as a reaction to the prevailing web's lightness, optimism, and frivolity.

Do not compress the contemporary record into a single canonical Bloomberg redesign without qualification. Reporting cited Bloomberg Businessweek features among prominent examples.

### 2019–2021: distribution infrastructure matures

- Figma Community entered public beta on October 22, 2019 as a public space for publishing live files that others could inspect, remix, and learn from.
- Tailwind announced an experimental just-in-time compiler on March 15, 2021 and moved it into core as an opt-in preview in version 2.1 on April 5, 2021, making arbitrary visual values easier to express.
- Webflow and Framer are described as reducing the distance between visual design and deployment, although Framer states that its professional site builder launched in May 2022, outside this source timeline window.

These capabilities created favorable distribution conditions. Their first-party sources do not claim they were built for neubrutalism, so causal attribution remains retrospective synthesis.

### 2021–2023: the style crystallizes

Michał Malewicz published “Neubrutalism is taking over the web” on March 29, 2022. The essay is a strong popularization milestone but not proof that he invented the style or coined the term first; it describes something already emerging.

The source identifies a progression from explicitly tagged portfolio work in 2022, through challenge and community circulation, to reusable interface kits in early 2023 and commercially packaged systems by mid-2023. Dated artifacts support the existence of that progression, while labels such as “crystallization” remain interpretive.

### 2024–present: taxonomy and infrastructure

The movement now appears as a searchable label across open-source repositories and template markets. GitHub has a dedicated topic containing libraries, design systems, templates, and style-focused projects. Webflow and Framer marketplaces contain neubrutalist assets.

Treat counts as volatile and verify them before use. The source's claim of thousands of Framer-tagged templates was not substantiated during the delegated source review and may conflate the marketplace's overall scale with style-specific inventory.

## The “patient zero” problem

No originator is universally accepted. The record consists of independently published essays, screenshots, tags, portfolio projects, live files, tutorials, kits, and marketplace products rather than a manifesto or organized movement.

Useful diffusion milestones include:

- March 29, 2022: Malewicz's widely circulated essay.
- Spring 2022: rapid public reuse of the term, though historical clustering on dynamic platforms needs archival evidence.
- Late 2022: tagged portfolio and challenge-related circulation.
- Early 2023: reusable interface kits.
- Mid-2023: commercial marketplace packaging.

These milestones do not prove invention. Search indexes change, tags may be added later, listings are updated, and early work may disappear. A defensible formulation is: **Malewicz supplied the best-documented early popularization event, while the visual style and possibly the label emerged across multiple platforms.**

## The Figma effect

Figma's importance in the source's account is distributive, not inventive. Community enabled live source files to circulate as editable components and systems rather than screenshots. Variables launched in June 2023 and supported reusable values, aliases, modes, themes, and token-like workflows. They cannot explain the earliest 2022 emergence or early-2023 kits, but they helped systematize a style already in circulation.

The central historical interpretation is a shift from first-wave web brutalism's anti-system stance to neubrutalism's compatibility with reusable values, components, kits, templates, and marketplaces. Reproducibility, inspectability, and distribution allowed the style to spread consistently. Treat this as analysis supported by platform capabilities, not a measured causal finding.

## Source quality

- Prefer Banham's 1955 essay, Malewicz's 2022 essay, official platform announcements, W3C standards, and live first-party listings for direct claims.
- Use MoMA and the Metropolitan Museum of Art as authoritative curatorial sources for Italian Radical Architecture and Memphis.
- Use contemporary reporting to document reception, not to prove origin.
- Use Wikipedia only for orientation where stronger sources exist.
- Label the Memphis-to-neubrutalism lineage, “patient zero” model, platform crystallization, and anti-system-to-system-friendly thesis as the source page's synthesis.
